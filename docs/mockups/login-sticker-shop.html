Sign-in page · Theme A

Two ways to sign in

Today /login opens with a big gradient card that just says “ask me anything”, then a stack of buttons. The page never says what signing in is for. These two versions lead with the reason — chatting back — and keep “I only want a link” visible without competing with it.

What the page must do

Sign in (Google / Apple), or skip it and take a link. Both in one screen, with sign-in clearly primary for returning people.

Reality of the buttons

Google and Apple draw their own buttons in production; the mockup shows their footprint and styling around them. The dev demo accounts stay, in a clearly marked local-only strip.

Same parts

BrandMark, .form-card / sticker cards, AppButton, the ink + pink tokens. Nothing new to build.

Notes for the build

What changes from today

  • The gradient hero card with “ask me anything” goes — it belongs on the public link page, not the sign-in page.
  • Headline becomes “welcome back.” and explains what sign-in unlocks.
  • “I just want a link” stops being a text line and becomes a real second door with the username preview.
  • Demo accounts move into a dashed, labelled local-only strip.

Provider buttons

Keep GoogleSignIn.vue / AppleSignIn.vue untouched — they render Google’s and Apple’s own widgets. Style the container: the mockup shows the footprint, the real widget drops into that slot. Where no client IDs are set, the existing “sign-in isn’t set up” line replaces them.

Behaviour that must survive

  • next query is honoured after sign-in.
  • A link-only guest can reach this page (already fixed) and keeps their username.
  • Error state: “We couldn’t sign you in” stays visible above the buttons.

Motion & a11y

Entrance rise only; the note in B tilts but never drifts. Both buttons ≥48px, ink focus rings, provider widgets keep their own accessible names, and the demo strip is hidden outside dev.
